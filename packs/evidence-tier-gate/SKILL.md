# Evidence tier gate

Rank the finding, not the reputation of the source.
- Tier 1: controlled research, systematic reviews, meta-analyses (Ekman hot spots and leakage, Vrij cognitive-load and content methods). Judge against the study conditions. Pooled human accuracy near 54% is context-bound history, not this system's accuracy.
- Tier 2: field heuristics (Hughes baselines, cluster scoring, C5 = Change, Context, Clusters, Culture, Checklist; BTE; SLATE). Keep detail, label validity as unverified.
- Tier 3: interpretive maps (Jung shadow and archetypes, Machiavelli and Greene power, Sun Tzu positioning and timing). Hypotheses, never diagnoses or proof.
- Tier 4: demonstrations (Brown, Pearlman, Banachek, Ericksonian suggestion). Explain mechanism, no mind-reading, no guaranteed compliance.

Gate steps:
1. State the defensive objective and the tier you can actually reach with the material given.
2. Flag any claim that exceeds its tier, including your own.
3. Othello Error check: a stress or deception-looking cue is also what an honest, frightened person shows. Name the honest alternative every time. No automatic lie verdicts. Never infer guilt from appearance.
4. Demographic labels are hypotheses, never individual verdicts.
5. Correct mathematics does not validate a behavioral model; say so when numbers are attached to a Tier 2 to 4 claim.
6. Scoring tables: if you work from a published cue-scoring table (for example a BTE, C5 or SLATE sheet), use your own licensed copy directly; do not rebuild it from memory. Where the table's thresholds conflict (for example a per-group count versus a per-Q&A-period count), show both, say which you used, and never silently choose. Cue scores are heuristics, never lie verdicts or calibrated probabilities.
